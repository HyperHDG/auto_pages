7/*!*************************************************************************************************
8 * \file timoshenko.hxx
9 * \brief Test problems for the Timoshenko beam solver on networks of straight beams.
10 *
11 * Notation of Hauck, Malqvist, Rupp, "Arbitrary order approximations at constant cost for
12 * Timoshenko beam network models" (2024), eq. (2.2): on an edge with unit tangent i, the
13 * displacement u, rotation r, stress resultant n and moment m satisfy, with the solver's unit
14 * coefficients C_n = C_m = I,
15 * -(d_x u + i x r) = n, -d_x r = m, d_x n = f, d_x m + i x n = g,
16 * with continuity of u, r and balance of n, m at the nodes. Conventions of the implementation:
17 * the rotation variable phi of the solver is -r (a rigid rotation u = omega x p goes with
18 * phi = -omega), right_hand_side_n is f and right_hand_side_m is -g. The parameter functions
19 * receive a point and a direction and return the component of the vector field along it.
20 **************************************************************************************************/
21
22/*!*************************************************************************************************
23 * \brief Manufactured solution on the cross of beams along the x and the y axis
24 * (domains/cross.geo; also the single beam along y of domains/line.geo).
25 *
26 * u = cos(pi x) e_z + cos(pi y) e_y and r = -(sin(pi x) e_y + sin(pi y) e_z), zero at the four
27 * ends. Beams along x (i = e_x): n = (pi + 1) sin(pi x) e_z, m = pi cos(pi x) e_y, so
28 * f = pi (pi + 1) cos(pi x) e_z and g = -(pi^2 + pi + 1) sin(pi x) e_y. Beams along y
29 * (i = e_y): n = sin(pi y) (pi e_y + e_x), m = pi cos(pi y) e_z, f = pi cos(pi y) (pi e_y + e_x)
30 * and g = -(pi^2 + 1) sin(pi y) e_z. Both beam lines are smooth through the junction, so the
31 * junction conditions hold.
32 **************************************************************************************************/
