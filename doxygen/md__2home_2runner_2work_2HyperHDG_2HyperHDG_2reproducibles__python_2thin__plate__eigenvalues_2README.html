Eigenvalue problems on networks of thin plates

This directory contains the numerical experiments of the manuscript

‍T. Chaumont-Frelet, M. Dwivedi, and A. Rupp, Eigenvalue problems on geometric hypergraphs: singular limits and HDG approximation.

Three plates of thicknesses (3/2, 3/2, 3) eps meet along a straight junction of cross section eps Y_N that carries the mass density rho = 4 mu / (39 eps). For eps -> 0, the eigenvalues of the diffusion operator on the thin domain converge to those of a limit problem on the hypergraph of three unit squares, whose junction carries the mass density mu = 24 / (17 pi). The plates are clamped at their far ends, and all other boundaries are natural. The experiments study

study what rates (theory)
hdg HDG method for the limit problem, degrees 0 to 3, uniform refinement h^(2p+1), h^(p+1)
thin thin domains for eps = 2^-3, ..., 2^-10 versus the limit problem eps, eps^(1/2)
combined thin-domain eigenvalues versus HDG eigenvalues (degree 1), all eps, h eps + h^(2p+1)

for the clusters lambda_1 (simple), lambda_2 = lambda_3 = pi^2 (double), and lambda_4 = 17 pi^2 / 16 (simple). The eigenvalue rates and the eigenspace rates (gap in the weighted mass space H_0) are listed in this order.

Running the experiments

Requirements: a C++20 compiler, CMake 3.18 or newer, LAPACK, Python 3.9 or newer with NumPy and SciPy, and the git submodules of HyperHDG (nanobind). Then,

git clone --recursive https://github.com/HyperHDG/HyperHDG.git
cd HyperHDG
python3 -m pip install numpy scipy cmake # if not available otherwise
python3 reproducibles_python/thin_plate_eigenvalues/run_experiments.py

(In an existing clone, git pull and git submodule update --init --recursive first.)

The C++ module is compiled on the first run (into .hyperhdg-cache/, which takes about a minute), and all studies take about seven minutes on a laptop. --only hdg thin combined selects studies, and --quick runs the coarsest levels only (a few seconds, without writing tables). The results are written to output/thin_plate_eigenvalues/results/ (ignored by git), or to the directory given by --output:

  • hdg.json, thin.json, combined.json: all computed eigenvalues, errors, gaps, residuals, and the numbers of unknowns,
  • table_hdg_eigenvalue_error.tex, table_hdg_gap.tex, table_thin_eigenvalue_error.tex, table_thin_gap.tex, table_combined.tex: the bodies of the tables in the manuscript,
  • environment.json: the versions of Python, NumPy, and SciPy, the platform, and the run times.

The subdirectory results/ of this directory contains the results of the manuscript (computed on macOS with Apple clang), which are the reference, and the main branch of HyperHDG reproduces them. At the end of a run, the new results are compared with the reference, file by file: identical files are reported as such, and for differing JSON files the largest absolute deviation is printed. Repeated runs on the same machine give identical results; other machines reproduce them up to rounding errors, which affect the eigenvalue errors of the HDG method below about 1e-11 and the thin-domain eigenvalues at about 1e-6. Since the result files contain no run times or machine information, they can also be compared directly, e.g. by diff -r reproducibles_python/thin_plate_eigenvalues/results output/thin_plate_eigenvalues/results (which only reports environment.json and the files of --quick runs). To update the reference, run the script with --output reproducibles_python/thin_plate_eigenvalues/results.

Files

  • limit.py: exact eigenpairs of the limit problem (by separation of variables).
  • plates.py: the hypergraph of three plates (a .geo file) and the evaluation of the HDG eigenvalue errors and eigenspace gaps.
  • thin.py: the thin domains. Since neither the cross section nor the coefficients depend on the direction of the junction, and both caps are natural boundaries, the eigenfunctions are w(x, y) cos(k pi (z + 1/2)), where w solves -div(grad w) + k^2 pi^2 w = lambda rho w on the T-shaped cross section. These problems are solved by the same HDG method (degree 5) on meshes that are graded towards the junction and towards the reentrant corners. The averaging map takes thickness averages on the plates (extended into the collar by reflection) and the average over the junction cross section. A second computation on refined meshes estimates the accuracy of the eigenvalues and of the averaged eigenspaces (gap between the averaged eigenspaces of both meshes, recorded in thin.json).
  • common.py: compilation of the module, .geo output (the generated domain files in output/thin_plate_eigenvalues/ are named after a hash of their content), assembly, eigensolver (Lanczos in shift and invert mode with diagonal equilibration, iterative refinement, a fixed start vector, and purification of the eigenvectors, since the mass matrix is singular), Gauss quadrature, and gaps between subspaces (sines of the principal angles from residuals of orthogonal projections of the sampled functions, accurate also for tiny gaps).
  • run_experiments.py: runs the studies, writes the results and tables, and compares them with the reference in results/.

The C++ part consists of LocalSolver::DiffusionEigsWeighted (include/HyperHDG/local_solver/diffusion_eigs_weighted_ldgh.hxx), which provides the local stiffness and mass matrices of the HDG method with eliminated flux (plate weights, densities, and hypernode mass are read from the hyperedge properties of the domain file), and GlobalLoop::GeneralizedEigenvalue (include/HyperHDG/global_loop/generalized_eigenvalue.hxx), which assembles them and evaluates discrete functions at quadrature points.
